What Breathizen is and how it works

Breathizen (printed as BreathiZen on the label) is a dietary supplement that comes as a liquid in an amber glass bottle with a pump sprayer. Instead of swallowing capsules, you spray it into your mouth once a day. The manufacturer describes it as a natural formula for clearer airways and easier breathing, and says it is made in the United States in an FDA-registered facility.

It is a supplement, not a medicine. It has not been evaluated by the FDA to treat any lung condition, and it is not a substitute for an inhaler or anything else your doctor has prescribed.

What is Breathizen?

A once-daily oral spray with five plant and enzyme ingredients: mullein leaf extract, ginger root extract, bromelain, organic lemon extract and organic cordyceps mushroom extract.

Why do people try Breathizen?

Most buyers are adults who feel stuffy or congested, cough up mucus in the morning, or get winded more easily than they used to, and who want a herbal option to add to their routine.

How does Breathizen work?

According to the maker, mullein and bromelain help loosen mucus, ginger calms irritated airways, lemon adds antioxidants, and cordyceps supports stamina. The evidence for each is covered below.

How to use Breathizen: four pumps each morning

The directions on the official Breathizen website are short. The whole routine takes a few seconds.

  1. Shake the bottle

    Shake well before every use so the extracts are mixed evenly.

  2. Aim at your tongue

    Open your mouth and point the nozzle at your tongue or the inside of your mouth.

  3. Press four pumps

    Four pumps make one serving, about 0.60 mL of liquid.

  4. Repeat once a day

    The maker suggests the morning, before your first meal. Do not take more than directed.

Give it time, and keep your usual care. Herbal supplements act gradually, if they act at all. Keep taking any prescribed medicine, and see a doctor promptly for shortness of breath, wheezing, chest pain or a cough that lasts more than three weeks.

Breathizen ingredients and the evidence behind them

Breathizen contains five active ingredients. Here is why each one is in the formula and what the research says about it.

Mullein leaf extract 10:1 concentrate A soothing herb, traditionally used to help loosen mucus and ease airway irritation. Mullein has been used for coughs and chest congestion for centuries. Modern research is mostly laboratory work, and human trials for breathing are scarce. See the MedlinePlus herb and supplement index.
Ginger root extract Included to help calm irritated airways and support circulation. Ginger is best studied for nausea. Research on airways is early and mostly from the lab. The NCCIH ginger fact sheet notes it is generally safe in food amounts but may interact with blood-thinning medicines.
Bromelain pineapple enzyme An enzyme included to help break down thick mucus. The NCCIH bromelain fact sheet reports limited evidence that it may ease nasal and sinus symptoms alongside standard treatment. Avoid it if you are allergic to pineapple.
Organic lemon extract Adds vitamin C and antioxidants to support normal immune function. Vitamin C contributes to normal immune function, according to the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements. The amount of vitamin C in a Breathizen serving is not published.
Organic cordyceps extract Cordyceps militaris fruiting body A mushroom extract included to support stamina and breathing endurance. A few small studies suggest modest effects on exercise tolerance. The evidence is preliminary, and results on PubMed are mixed.

Breathing habits to pair with Breathizen

No spray replaces the basics. These four habits have far stronger evidence than any supplement, and they cost little or nothing.

Practice slow breathing

Pursed-lip and belly breathing help you empty your lungs fully. The American Lung Association suggests five to ten minutes a day. The pacer at the top of this page uses the same slow rhythm.

Quit smoking and vaping

Nothing does more for your lungs. Free coaching, text programs and quit plans are available at Smokefree.gov.

Clean up indoor air

Dust, smoke, mold and strong fumes irritate airways. The EPA's indoor air quality guide covers ventilation, filters and common sources.

Stay active and hydrated

Regular movement trains your breathing muscles, and fluids keep mucus thin. The National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute explains how the lungs work.
